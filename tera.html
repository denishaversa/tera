Sustainability doesn't need another dashboard.It needs a decision layer.

Organizations increasingly have access to sustainability data across waste, energy, carbon, water, supply chains, and other operational areas. Having more data does not make the next decision obvious.

Tera is designed to become the decision layer between sustainability data and organizational action. It evaluates potential initiatives across four lenses, then surfaces recommendations, evidence, assumptions, and trade-offs. The final decision stays with the organization.

  • Environmental impact
  • Financial feasibility
  • Organizational priorities
  • Implementation feasibility

Why Tera?

The name evokes terra, Earth, while keeping the concise, technology-oriented character of a modern software product.

Organizations operate within interconnected environmental, financial, and operational systems. Sustainability decisions cannot be made using environmental impact alone. Tera helps organizations understand those relationships and make more informed choices.

Better decisions for the systems we depend on.

How Tera works

  1. 1

    Understand

    Bring together sustainability, operational, financial, and organizational information.

  2. 2

    Evaluate

    Assess potential interventions across environmental impact, cost, feasibility, and organizational priorities.

  3. 3

    Prioritize

    Compare potential actions to identify the opportunities that warrant further consideration.

  4. 4

    Decide

    Review recommendations, evidence, assumptions, and trade-offs, then make the final call.

Why Tera surfaced this. The intervention may provide comparatively high waste-diversion potential while requiring less capital and organizational change than infrastructure-heavy alternatives.

Evidence and assumptions

  • Contamination in recycling and compost streams is a common cause of low diversion, and better sorting targets it directly.
  • Assumes existing hauler contracts already accept the sorted material streams.
  • Assumes signage and bin changes can be phased within the 12-month timeline.
  • Diversion gains depend on participation, so an employee program is a likely companion initiative.
